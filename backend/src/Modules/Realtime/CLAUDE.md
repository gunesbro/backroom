# Realtime (cell)

Contract: [docs/contracts/phase-1.md](../../../../docs/contracts/phase-1.md) ("Identity and tokens", "Live events"). Technical module (no Domain project; no tables except an inbox).

## Responsibility

- SignalR hub `/hubs/realtime` in the `app` role, MessagePack protocol, Valkey backplane; `CloseOnAuthenticationExpiration` so connections end with their token.
- Groups (prefix `cell:{cellId}:org:{orgId}`): the organization (content-free events), `:conversation:{id}` (any kind; threads use the parent's) and `:account:{accountId}` (per-person events, only that account's connections). `SubscribeOrganization` (joins the organization and the account group) / `SubscribeConversation` check membership and read access.
- `ILiveEventPublisher` (Realtime.Contracts) for modules, used after commit.
- Revoked sessions: `ISessionRevocation` (Realtime.Contracts) stores the session or account in Valkey until `keep_until`, closes matching connections on all nodes, and backs the authentication check that rejects revoked tokens on HTTP and hub connects.
- Consumes `organization.member-removed.v1` and `messaging.conversation-member-removed.v1` to remove the account's connections from that organization's groups or that conversation's group on every node; `ILiveEventPublisher.RemoveFromConversationAsync` does the latter right after Messaging's commit.

## Owns

- Schema `realtime` with only an `inbox` (the `member-removed` consumer). Connection, group and revoked-session state live in SignalR and Valkey.

## Rules

- Live events never go through RabbitMQ and never wait for it.
- Payloads are versioned by event name (`MessagePosted`, later `MessagePosted.v2`), because clients may talk to cells on different versions. `MessagePosted.v2` (`MessagePostedV2Payload`: version and change numbers and the cell epoch) is published by Messaging in place of `MessagePosted` when `Features:MessageSync` is on (Phase 2 step 5).
- Message text is in live events but never in logs; the `access_token` query parameter is redacted.

## Status

- Implemented (Phase 1 step 6.2): `Backroom.Modules.Realtime.Contracts` (payloads with MessagePack `[Key("camelCase")]` attributes, `ILiveEventPublisher`, `LiveEventNames`) and `Backroom.Modules.Realtime.Infrastructure` (technical module: hub, gateway and publisher in one project, because the publisher sends through the hub's context; no Presentation project). Schema `realtime` holds only the consumer's `inbox`.
- **Hub** `/hubs/realtime` (`MapRealtimeHub`, app role): authenticated, MessagePack only (`SupportedProtocols = ["messagepack"]`; SignalR always registers the JSON protocol, but the handshake refuses it), `CloseOnAuthenticationExpiration`, explicit limits (`Realtime` options: receive size 4 KB, 600 groups per connection (Phase 2; an organization subscription takes two), 20 connections per account per node, keep-alive 15 s, client timeout 30 s, handshake 10 s, one invocation at a time per client, WebSockets only; 60 subscribe calls per 10 s and 10 refused ones per connection, after which the connection is aborted with `realtime.rate_limited` / `realtime.too_many_refusals`). Methods `SubscribeOrganization`/`UnsubscribeOrganization`/`SubscribeConversation`/`UnsubscribeConversation` (Phase 2 step 2 replaced the channel methods); refusals are `HubException`s with the stable codes `realtime.not_found` (non-member, other organization, unknown, archived or unreadable conversation: the same code for all, so nothing leaks), `realtime.invalid_argument`, `realtime.subscription_limit`, `realtime.unavailable`.
- **Checks:** the organization comes from the argument, is entered in the hub invocation's scope and accepted only after `IOrganizationMembershipCheck`; for a conversation `IConversationReadAccess` (Messaging.Contracts: an open public channel, or an open conversation the account is a member of) is checked in the same transaction under RLS. The token's `sub` is the account (`AccountUserIdProvider`).
- **Publisher:** `ValkeyLiveEventPublisher` sends to the SignalR group (`cell:{cellId}:org:{orgId}:conversation:{conversationId}` / `cell:{cellId}:org:{orgId}`); the Valkey backplane (`Microsoft.AspNetCore.SignalR.StackExchangeRedis`) delivers it to connections on every node. Bounded: waits at most `PublishTimeout` (250 ms, because the sending request waits for it; the multiplexer fails fast while Valkey is down), at most `MaxConcurrentPublishes` (256) in flight, further events are dropped (never queued), failures are logged with ids and the exception type and counted, and nothing reaches the caller.
- **Valkey connection:** `ValkeyConnection` (one multiplexer per process, `Valkey:ConnectionString`, `AbortOnConnectFail=false` so the cell starts when Valkey is down) with the pub/sub channel prefix `backroom:cell:{cellId}:` (development cells share one server). The AppHost passes `Valkey__ConnectionString` to the cell's app and worker and pins the image (`ValkeyImageTag`, same as the tests' `ValkeyServer`).
- **Member removal across nodes.** A node only knows its own connection ids, so removal is a broadcast: the worker's `MemberRemovedConsumer` (`organization.member-removed.v1`, inbox in schema `realtime`) publishes the two ids (32 bytes) on the Valkey channel `realtime:member-removed`; every app node's `GroupRemovalListener` receives it and removes its own connections of that account from every group of that organization, the account group included (`ConnectionRegistry`: connection → account and groups; state exists only while the connection is open, at most 600 groups per connection and 20 connections per account). The consumer throws when Valkey does not accept the message, so RabbitMQ redelivers; removing twice is harmless. Each connection has a gate held from the membership check to the group join (and by the removal), so a subscribe cannot slip past a removal. Pub/sub is at most once: a node that missed the message keeps the connection in the group only until its token expires (`CloseOnAuthenticationExpiration`, five minutes), and the reconnect has to pass the membership check again. Messages are handled in order, one at a time; they come at the rate of members being removed, so the listener has no queue worth bounding.
- **Token:** the cell's JWT validation (audience `cell-{cellId}`) accepts `access_token` from the query string on the hub path only (`AddAccessTokenAuthentication(..., queryTokenPath)`); elsewhere a token in a URL is refused. The query token is kept out of logs by turning off the hosting `Request starting/finished` lines (`Microsoft.AspNetCore.Hosting.Diagnostics` at Warning, set by the same call) and out of traces by `QueryRedaction` (ServiceDefaults, `url.query` tag). A test captures every log line of a connected client.
- **Metrics** (meter `Backroom.Realtime`): `backroom.realtime.connections`, `.subscriptions`, `.publishes`, `.publish_failures` (tags `event`, `reason`), `.rejected_subscriptions`.
- Tests (`Backroom.Modules.Realtime.IntegrationTests`): two app nodes on one Valkey (Testcontainers), a message posted through node A reaches node B; subscribe checks; member removal on both nodes; token expiry (a token the test signs with a second trusted key, because Keycloak's live for five minutes); JSON refused; limits; no token in logs; publish failure with Valkey down.
- **No producer yet:** `MemberRemovedConsumer` has nothing to consume until Organization publishes `organization.member-removed.v1` when member removal ships.
- **Deploy phase:** Valkey carries the SignalR backplane and the removal channel, so anyone who can reach it can inject live events into any group and make nodes drop subscriptions. It needs ACLs (a user limited to pub/sub and the keys the backplane uses), TLS and network isolation (only the cell's nodes), and the connection string stays a secret.
- Not yet: the Organization module publishing `organization.member-removed.v1` (the event record `MemberRemoved` is in Organization.Contracts; nothing sends it yet), private channels and DMs (the read check grows with them), the web client.
- Modules call the publisher after their unit of work has committed and never let a failure reach the caller (Messaging's `AfterCommitLiveEvents`).

## Conversation and account groups (Phase 2 step 2)

**Human review required** (privacy: who receives which live events).

- **Hub methods** `SubscribeConversation(orgId, conversationId)` / `UnsubscribeConversation` replace the channel methods (contract docs/contracts/phase-2.md, "Live events"). The check is `IConversationReadAccess`, so a private conversation, DM or group DM is joined only by its open members (Owner and Admin roles included: no membership, `realtime.not_found`). `MessagePosted` goes to the conversation group (payload unchanged until `MessagePosted.v2`).
- **Account group** `cell:{cellId}:org:{orgId}:account:{accountId}`: `SubscribeOrganization` joins it with the organization group, under the same membership check, for the token's account only (never an argument), so it holds only that account's connections. `UnsubscribeOrganization` leaves both. It is for `SeenChanged`, `ReadStateChanged` and the other per-person events of later steps; nothing sends to it yet.
- **Limits** unchanged in kind and adapted: 600 groups per connection (an organization subscription needs room for two, or neither is joined), 60 subscribe calls per 10 s, 10 refusals then abort.
- **Removal from a conversation** (leave now; removal, DMs and group DMs in later steps): Messaging calls `ILiveEventPublisher.RemoveFromConversationAsync` after its commit (best effort, `PublishTimeout`, never throws, counted as `publish_failures` with `event=ConversationMemberRemoved`), and the worker's `ConversationMemberRemovedConsumer` repeats it from `messaging.conversation-member-removed.v1` (throws when Valkey refuses, so RabbitMQ redelivers). Both publish the three ids (48 bytes) on `realtime:conversation-member-removed`; every app node's `GroupRemovalListener` removes its connections of that account from that conversation's group only, under each connection's gate. Idempotent. If both broadcasts are lost, the bound is the token lifetime (5 minutes, owner decision 12); reads refuse at once. Leaving a public channel also leaves its group; a client that keeps it open subscribes again (public channels stay readable).
- **Removal from the organization** removes every group of that organization (organization, account, conversation groups), unchanged path.
- **Late durable removal:** `ConversationMemberRemovedConsumer` checks `IConversationReadAccess.IsMemberAsync` (an open membership, not read access) in its unit of work first and skips the eviction when the account is a member again (it rejoined), so a backlog or redelivery never cuts off a current member; an account that left an open public channel is still evicted. A rejoin between that check and the broadcast is not covered (the client resubscribes on its next reconnect).
- **Window after the commit** (contract, "Live events"): eviction runs on its own Valkey subscription, apart from the backplane, so an event published milliseconds after the removal commit can still reach the removed connections; there is no delivery-time membership check. `@backroom/realtime` drops conversation events for conversations it no longer wants (own leave, `MembershipChanged` removed). Removal by others must not ship until there is a delivery-time check or the owner has accepted the window again.
- **All-or-nothing subscribe:** when a join fails, the groups the same call joined are left again (an organization subscription never holds the organization group without the account group); if leaving fails too, the connection is closed.
- **Forged removal messages:** anyone who can publish on the Valkey removal channels can only take connections out of groups, never put them in (joining always goes through the hub's checks), so the worst case is a denial of service (people stop getting live events until they resubscribe), not a leak. The deploy-phase Valkey ACL and isolation above apply.
- Tests: `ConversationGroupTests` (removal on both nodes by both paths, other accounts untouched, account-group isolation on both nodes, account group left after organization removal, resubscribe refused after leaving, subscribe racing a removal under the gate, late removal after a rejoin; trait `Category=Privacy`), `SubscriptionTests` (private conversation refused for non-members on both nodes), `LimitTests` (the organization subscription needs two places).

## Revoked sessions (Phase 1 step 6.3)

**Human review required** (tokens and identity).

- **Contract** `ISessionRevocation.RevokeAsync(SessionRevocation { AccountId, SessionId?, RevokedAt, KeepUntil })` (Realtime.Contracts), called by the connector for `SessionRevoked` and `FullSync`; registered in every role. It validates (account id, session id at most 128 characters, `keep_until` not before `revoked_at` and at most a day after it; else `ArgumentException`), ignores an expired revocation, then (1) stores it in Valkey, (2) takes it into the node's cache, (3) publishes it on `realtime:session-revoked`. Valkey failures throw, so the link retries. Idempotent.
- **Storage**: keys `backroom:cell:{cellId}:revoked:sid:{accountId}:{sid}` and `...:revoked:account:{accountId}` (a session belongs to its account; the cell id keeps cells on one server apart), value `{revokedAtMs}:{keepUntilMs}`, expiry at `keep_until` (at most `Realtime:RevocationMaxRetention`, 1 h). The set is bounded by the sign-outs of the last 5.5 minutes and cleans itself. An entry that cannot be parsed counts as revoked for a minute (fail closed).
- **Closing connections**: every app node's `SessionRevocationListener` takes the revocation into its cache and, for its own connections of the account (`ConnectionRegistry`; `ConnectionState` now knows the token's `sid` and `iat` and the hub's abort), sends `SessionRevoked { reason: "session_ended" }` to the connection, schedules the abort `RevocationCloseGrace` (250 ms) later on a `TimeProvider` timer (not awaited, at most 10,000 pending; the next revocation is learned without delay). A session id closes that session's connections; a revocation of every session closes those whose token was issued up to the second of `revoked_at`. Pub/sub is at most once: a node that missed it refuses the token within the cache TTL, and a connection it left open ends with its token (at most 5 minutes). The hub also checks a connection right after registering it, so a revocation that arrived between the request check and the registration is not missed.
- **Check on every request and hub connect**: `RevocationMiddleware` (`app.UseRevokedSessionCheck()`, after `UseAuthentication`, app role) reads `sid`, `sub`, `iat` of the authenticated token. Revoked: `401` `identity.session_revoked` with `WWW-Authenticate: Bearer error="invalid_token"`. A token without `iat` counts as issued at the epoch (any account revocation covers it). Account revocation: refused when `iat <= floor(revoked_at)` (seconds); a sign-in after it works.
- **Latency and cost**: `RevocationChecker` keeps bounded in-memory maps (`RevocationCacheMaxEntries` 100,000 per kind, sized for 5,000 users; lock-free lookups) in front of Valkey: revoked entries (few, kept until `keep_until`, never evicted while in force) and clear ones live apart; over the limit the oldest clear answers are evicted in insertion order, never everything. A revoked entry lasts until `keep_until`, a clear one `RevocationCacheTtl` (5 s). One `MGET` per active session per 5 s, no Valkey call per request. **A revocation is enforced on HTTP and at hub connect at once on nodes that got the broadcast and within 5 s otherwise** (after the cell stored it; the link's delivery adds about a second), and on open connections at once through the broadcast, at the latest when the token expires.
- **Valkey down: fail closed (default), decision for the human review.** A read that fails is answered from a clear entry up to `RevocationStaleTolerance` (30 s) older than its TTL, so a Valkey restart does not reject people; after that, and for sessions never seen, the check says "unavailable": `503` `realtime.revocation_check_unavailable` with `Retry-After` (never `401`: clients sign out on `401`). A failed read is remembered for `RevocationOutageBackoff` (1 s), so a hung Valkey costs one `CommandTimeout` per second for the node, not per request; with the fail-fast backlog a refused connection answers at once. Reasons: no revocation can be stored or announced during the outage (the connector retries), so serving on a guess would let a revoked session through for the whole outage, up to its token's lifetime; revoked entries already known are enforced without Valkey. Cost: while Valkey is down for more than ~35 s the app role answers `503` to authenticated requests of sessions it has not just seen (live events and the backplane need Valkey anyway). `Realtime:RevocationFailClosed=false` switches to fail open (the request is served and counted, metric `backroom.realtime.revocation_checks_unavailable` tag `outcome=allowed`); it bounds the exposure to the token lifetime (5 minutes). The owner decides which of the two Phase 1 ships (recorded in the contract, decision 14).
- **Metrics**: `backroom.realtime.revoked_requests`, `.revocation_checks_unavailable` (tag `outcome`), `.revoked_connections`.
- **Deploy phase**: Valkey ACL must allow the keys `backroom:cell:{id}:revoked:*` and the channel for the nodes only; anyone who can write them can lock people out or (by deleting keys) un-revoke within the cache TTL.
- Tests (`Backroom.Modules.Realtime.IntegrationTests`): `RevocationCheckerTests` (fake store and clock: cache TTL, push, stale tolerance, fail closed, backoff, bounds, account revocations), `SessionRevocationTests` (two nodes on a real Valkey, own-signed and real Keycloak tokens: 401 on both nodes, hub connect refused, `SessionRevoked` received and connections closed on both nodes, other sessions untouched, expiry by `keep_until`, a node started later, cells kept apart), `RevocationOutageTests` (dead Valkey: `503` quickly, fail open by setting, revoking throws).

## Presence on the hub (Phase 2 step 28)

**Human review required** (privacy). Contract: [phase-2.md](../../../../docs/contracts/phase-2.md), flow 9 and "Live events".

- Methods `WatchPresence(orgId, accountIds)` (active member; replaces the connection's watch set; at most 200 ids; ids of people who are not active members there are dropped without a word; counts as a subscribe call) and `ReportActivity("active" | "idle")` (at most one per 10 s per connection, extras dropped). Both answer `realtime.not_found` while `Features:Presence` is off. Connect and disconnect go to `IPresenceTracker` (Presence.Contracts; a no-op fallback when the Presence module is not added).
- Groups `cell:{cellId}:org:{orgId}:presence:{accountId}`, tracked per connection with their organization, so member removal drops them too. Limits: 600 groups per connection (conversations plus presence), receive size 16 KB (200 ids).
- `PresenceChanged { orgId, accountId, state, dndUntil?, customStatus? }` (timestamps as ISO 8601 strings) through `ILiveEventPublisher` to the person's presence group.
- The member-removed consumer also calls `IPresenceMemberships.ForgetAsync`, so the person's presence stops reaching that organization at once.
