## Goal and gate

The roadmap's Phase 2: DMs and group DMs, threads, replies, forwarding, reactions, read state, polls, scheduled messages, reminders; files, search, command palette, notifications, presence, audit log, moderation. Private channels, the rich text editor and the sidebar (sections, All DMs) come with them, because Phase 1 left them out and the Phase 2 features need them.

**Gate:** an end-to-end test on two cells and two clients per person passes these scenarios, and the Phase 2 k6 test meets the budgets below at 5,000 concurrent users:

1. Alice and Bob exchange DMs; Carol (Admin) can see neither the DM nor its existence. A group DM of three is converted to a private channel; Carol sees at most the total number of private channels in the organization, never a name, member or content, in no API, live event, search result, notification or audit entry.
2. A reply in a thread whose root is 150 messages back reaches Bob's second device after it was offline; edits, deletes and reactions made meanwhile are present after sync; unread counts and the "new" line match on both devices; "mark unread" on one device wins over an older automatic read on the other.
3. A file uploaded in a private channel downloads through a short-lived signed URL; after the person is removed from the channel the next download is refused and the local cache drops the channel.
4. Search finds `İstanbul` for `istanbul`, `ISTANBUL` and `ıstanbul`, only in conversations the searcher can read, and hit counts do not change when unreadable matches exist.
5. A mention reaches Bob as a notification; with "Do not disturb" on it lands in the bell silently.
6. An anonymous poll never reveals who voted for what, and the mapping is gone after it closes.
7. A scheduled message is sent at its time; a reminder fires at its time.
8. A reported message reaches moderators as a report with metadata only (owner decision 14); the report is in the audit log without content.
9. With `visibleHistoryDays` set, messages older than the boundary (and files attached only to them) appear in no read path, a client that cached them drops them at the boundary without treating them as missing, and they reappear at once when the limit is lifted; with `fileQuotaBytes` set, two concurrent uploads cannot exceed the quota.

**Not in Phase 2:** link previews and integrations (Phase 5, owner decision 3), work hours, leave and status history (Phase 4; Phase 2 uses the default 22:00–07:00 off-hours window), rooms and meetings in the command palette and the bell's Meetings and Rooms tabs (Phase 3), role and permission screens (Phase 5), plans and Licensing (a later phase; Phase 2 has no storage quota, owner decision 4), data export and account deletion, custom emoji, legal hold, mobile.

## Sequence numbers (ADR 0011, ADR 0012)

ADR 0011 fixes the observable guarantees, and [ADR 0012](../adr/0012-durable-sends-and-the-wal-flush.md) keeps its mechanism. This contract depends only on these guarantees:

- Every conversation has `change_seq` (every change takes the next number), `last_message_seq` (the newest message's `seq`), and every message has `seq` (creation order) and `version_seq` (its latest change).
- Numbers are positive 64-bit integers, increasing in commit order within one conversation; gaps are allowed; a cursor never skips a committed change.
- Clients treat them as opaque per-conversation cursors and never assume density.
- **Every cursor carries the cell epoch** (ADR 0011). Every response and live event that contains `seq`, `versionSeq`, `changeSeq`, `lastReadSeq`, `seenSeq` or a feed cursor also carries `epoch` (a string of at most 32 characters). Every request that takes one (`before`, `after`, `changed_after`, read-state `lastReadSeq`, the sync cursor) also takes `epoch`; a missing or different epoch, or a number ahead of the conversation's counter, answers `409 messaging.resync_required`, and the client runs a full resync of that cell. The sync cursor embeds the epoch itself.

- **Unconfirmed epoch.** While the cell has no confirmed epoch (flow 3, "Cell epoch"), no sequence number can be handed out, so reads that carry one (`GET /conversations/{id}/messages`) also answer `503 cell.epoch_unconfirmed` with `Retry-After`, like fenced writes.
- **Rollout flag.** `Features:MessageSync` (off until the clients send the epoch, step 15) guards the two parts that would break a client that does not know the epoch: with it on, `before` and `after` need `epoch`, and a send announces `MessagePosted.v2` in place of `MessagePosted`. `changed_after` always needs `epoch`, and a presented epoch is always checked.

- **History boundary.** When an organization limits visible history (flow 12), every message read also returns, per conversation, `historyBoundary { visibleFrom, visibleFromSeq, reason: "plan" }`: messages with `seq` below `visibleFromSeq` exist but are hidden. A read whose cursor or page reaches the boundary stops there with `historyBoundaryReached: true`, never `resync_required`; every conversation entry in sync carries the boundary; clients hide or drop cached messages below it and never treat them as missing. Nothing is deleted, so ADR 0011's tombstones stay permanent and its cursors stay valid. The boundary moves with time and with plan changes (`HistoryBoundaryChanged`).

**Mechanism and durability (ADR 0012, accepted).** A change is one transaction with a synchronous commit: it is acknowledged, and its live event is sent, only after the commit is durable. Numbers are assigned in commit order under the conversation row lock (`UPDATE … SET change_seq = change_seq + 1 … RETURNING`, held until the commit; ADR 0011), so changes to one conversation are serialized from that statement to their commit. Nothing about this changes in Phase 2. Because an acknowledgment cannot be faster than the WAL flush, cell storage must flush the WAL with p99 at most 10 ms; that is a deployment requirement, checked with the flush probe in `load-tests/README.md`, not something the send path can make up for.

## Flows

### 1. Conversations, membership and the sidebar

- **Kinds.** `public_channel`, `private_channel`, `dm`, `group_dm`, `thread` are all conversations (ADR 0011). Phase 1's `channels` become `conversations` (step 1).
- **Channel names.** Public channel names are unique among the organization's open public channels (`409 messaging.channel_name_taken`). Private channel names are not unique at all, and no check of a public channel's name looks at private channels, so neither creating nor renaming any channel reveals that a private channel exists; the client warns when a channel the person can see has the same name. Converting a group DM needs no unique name either.
- **Public channels.** Anyone with `channels.create-public` creates one. Every active member can read it, search it and join it; joined channels appear in the sidebar and count unread. Leaving is always allowed. Rename, description (at most 250 characters, `DisplayText` rules without the visible-character requirement; the 250 user-perceived characters are the application's limit, and the database `CHECK` only bounds the column at 1,000 code points, four per character) and archive need `channels.manage-public`. Archived conversations are read-only and stay readable and searchable.
- **Private channels.** Created with `channels.create-private`, shown with a lock. Only members see them (name, content, members). The creator is the owner: removes members, renames, archives, transfers ownership. When the owner leaves, ownership passes to the member who joined earliest (ties: lowest account id). Every member can add active organization members (at most 50 per call). Admins and the Owner role, without membership, never see a private channel's name, members or content (CLAUDE.md privacy rule: "Private channel names and content, DMs and group DMs are visible only to members; they never leak into the audit log, search or notifications"). The only admin view is the **total number** of private channels in the organization (`GET .../private-channels/count`), an aggregate with no per-channel rows, ids or member counts. The requirements allow admins to see each channel's existence and member count ("yalnızca var olduğunu ve üye sayısını görür"), so a total is within them; per-channel rows are deliberately left out. A private channel cannot become public.
- **DMs.** `POST .../dms { accountIds }` with one other active member opens the DM, creating it on first use (one per pair, unique key). A DM with a deactivated member stays readable and refuses new messages (`409 messaging.conversation_read_only`). A DM with oneself is not in Phase 2.
- **Group DMs.** 3 to 9 participants including the creator (owner decision 7). No name; shown by participants' names. The same set of people opens the existing group (unique key over the sorted set while nobody has left). Anyone can leave; nobody can be removed. Adding a person creates a new group without the old messages. "Özel kanala dönüştür" converts the group in place into a private channel (name required, `channels.create-private`, the converter becomes owner, history kept, members unchanged).
- **Threads.** A thread is created by the first reply to a root message of a channel, DM or group DM (not of a thread, a poll or a deleted message). Read access is the parent's. "Kanala da gönder" also posts a reference row in the parent conversation that shows the reply (edits and delete reflect).
- **Removal.** Being removed from a conversation or the organization, or leaving, removes read access at once (next request), removes the person's connections from the conversation's live group on every node, and appears in sync as a missing membership, after which the client deletes that conversation's messages and files from its local cache (ADR 0011).
- **Sidebar (personal).** Sections are personal and private (nobody else sees them): create, rename, delete, order, collapse; drag a conversation into a section; per section "Göster" (all, unread only), "Sırala" (recent activity, name A–Z), "Kenar çubuğunda en fazla" (5 for DMs by default; 10, 15, 20, all). Unread and starred conversations always show regardless of the limit and of collapsing; muted ones are faded and sort last. Per conversation: star, mute (1 hour, 8 hours, until tomorrow 09:00 in the person's time zone, until I unmute), hide (until a new message arrives), move to section. Fixed entries Threads and Saved sit at the top; Rooms and Calendar stay "Yakında".
- **All DMs.** Search by participant name and filters Tümü, Okunmamış, Gruplar, Kişiler, Yıldızlı, Sessizde; each row shows the newest visible message as preview (at most 120 characters, read under membership; flow 12) and offers star, mark read, mute, hide.

### 2. Messages

- **Rich text.** Formatting toolbar ("Aa"), bold, italic, strike, link, bulleted and numbered lists, quote, inline code, code block, mentions. Enter sends, Shift+Enter adds a line; the send button is disabled while empty; the thread composer is smaller and starts with the toolbar closed. The editor is Lexical (owner decision 1; license, release date and size are checked in its PR).
- **Input and storage.** The client sends `blocks` in our own JSON schema (Phase 1 decision 12), never editor-native or HTML. The server validates them against a closed schema (node types, marks, nesting depth at most 4, at most 32 KB of JSON (counted as in "Block schema"), `href` only `https`, `http` or `mailto` and at most 2,048 characters, text nodes under Phase 1's text rules), drops nothing silently (`400 messaging.message_invalid` with a `reason`), re-serializes them canonically and derives `text` (1 to 4,000 characters as in Phase 1, after derivation) and the mention and link rows. Unknown node types are refused on write and skipped on read.
- **Block schema (exact).** `blocks` is a JSON array of block nodes; every node is an object with a `type` string and the members below, and a member not listed is refused (`400 messaging.message_invalid`, `reason` names it). The writer is the web client and the server's canonical form is the same shape.
  - **Blocks** (the array's items): `{ "type": "paragraph" | "quote" | "code_block", "children": [text | mention …] }` and `{ "type": "bulleted_list" | "numbered_list", "children": [list_item …] }`. There is no heading block, no nested list and no table. A list has at least one item.
  - **`list_item`**: `{ "type": "list_item", "children": [text | mention …] }`, only inside a list.
  - **`text`**: `{ "type": "text", "text": "…", "marks": [mark …] }`. `text` is a non-empty string; a line break inside a block is a `\n` in `text` (there is no line-break node). Control characters other than `\n` and `\t` and lone surrogates are refused. `marks` may be omitted and means no marks; when present it is not empty, holds each type at most once and is ordered `bold`, `italic`, `strike`, `code`, `link`. The canonical form omits an empty `marks`, and merges neighbouring text nodes that have equal marks; an empty text node is refused like everything else outside the schema (`text.text`), so the client drops empty runs before sending. In a `code_block` text nodes carry no marks.
  - **Marks**: `{ "type": "bold" }`, `{ "type": "italic" }`, `{ "type": "strike" }`, `{ "type": "code" }` and `{ "type": "link", "href": "…" }`. A link is a mark, not a node; its only attribute is `href` (absolute `https`, `http` or `mailto`, at most 2,048 characters; the client sends the URL parser's canonical form).
  - **`mention`** (inline, wherever a text node may stand, no marks): `{ "type": "mention", "accountId": "<uuid>" }`, `{ "type": "mention", "groupId": "<uuid>" }` or `{ "type": "mention", "special": "everyone" | "here" }`, exactly one of the three members. The web client does not write mentions until the mention step; a reader that cannot show one skips it.
  - **Empty blocks.** A `paragraph` may have empty `children` only between other blocks (blank line); the first and last block are never an empty or whitespace-only paragraph. `quote`, `code_block` and `list_item` with no text are refused. A message needs at least one non-whitespace character after derivation.
  - **Depth.** The array counts as level 1, so the deepest path (array, list, `list_item`, `text`) is 4 levels, which is the limit.
  - **Derived `text`.** One line per `paragraph`, `quote` and `code_block` (its text nodes concatenated, `\n` kept) and one line per `list_item`, joined with `\n`; no list markers, no quote or code fences, link addresses not added, a mention contributes `@` and the name the server looks up (`@kanal` and `@burada` for the special ones). The server then trims (as .NET does), normalizes to NFC and converts `\r\n` and `\r` to `\n` **once, on the whole derived text**, and counts the 4,000 characters on that. It must not trim or NFC-normalize individual text nodes (that would change what the client counted) and it stores the nodes' `text` as received.
  - **Size.** The 32 KB limit is the UTF-8 byte length of the `blocks` value **as received in the request body**, counted on the raw bytes before parsing and not on a re-serialization, so the server's escaping or canonical form can neither inflate nor shrink it. The client measures its compact `JSON.stringify` (no spaces) in UTF-8, which is what it sends.
  - **Settled by the implementation (step 6).** `reason` is a fixed token naming the place, never text from the request: `blocks`, `blocks.size`, `blocks.empty`, `blocks.encoding`, `blocks.disabled`, `block.type`, `block.children`, `<node>.unknown_member`, `inline.duplicate_member`, `text.text`, `text.marks`, `mark.type`, `link.href`, `code_block.marks`, `paragraph.blank_edge`, `<block>.empty`, `list.children`, `mention`, `mention.account_id`, `mention.group_id`, `mention.special`, `links.limit`, `text.empty`, `text.length`, `text`, `client_message_id`. A message links to at most 50 distinct `http`/`https` addresses (`links.limit`). A node's `text` holds no `\r` (a line break is `\n`) and no line or paragraph separator. An `href` is ASCII without spaces (the URL parser's canonical form), with the scheme in lower case. With `blocks` present a `text` sent along is not read; without `blocks` a plain `text` send works as in Phase 1 and the server derives one paragraph. While `Features:RichText` is off a send with `blocks` is refused (`blocks.disabled`), not stripped. Reads and the answer to a send carry `blocks` as stored (`jsonb`: key order and spacing are not kept).
  - **Link safety.** The server checks only the form of an `href`: its host may be loopback, link-local or private (`127.0.0.1`, `169.254.169.254`, an internal name) and the address may carry userinfo, and `message_links.host` stores that host as written. Nothing on the server fetches a link in Phase 2. The link preview step (Phase 5) must resolve every host itself and refuse private, loopback and link-local ranges, redirects into them included (SSRF), and never trust `message_links.host`. Clients render link text with bidirectional isolation and always show the real address (on hover or focus, and before opening), because text may contain bidirectional controls: isolates (U+2066 to U+2069) are legitimate in mixed-direction text and are accepted. **Open question:** whether to refuse the override characters (U+202A to U+202E) in node text; step 6 accepts them.
- **Mentions.** Inline `mention` nodes name an `accountId`, a `groupId`, or `everyone` (`@kanal`) or `here` (`@burada`). Accounts must be active organization members and groups must exist (`400 messaging.mention_invalid`); `everyone`/`here` need `messages.mention-everyone`, groups `messages.mention-groups` (`403 permissions.forbidden`). At most 50 mentions per message (`400 messaging.mention_limit`). A mentioned person who cannot read the conversation gets nothing; the client offers to add them ("kanala ekle?") before sending, never the server. Group mentions follow ADR 0011: `everyone` is one row in the group mention list; `here` and named groups are expanded at send time (`here` to members online at that moment, capped at 1,000 feed rows; larger groups count only through the list). Step 6 stores a `message_mentions` row only for mentioned accounts that can read the conversation when the message is sent, and refuses group mentions (`groupId`, `everyone`, `here`) with `400 messaging.mention_invalid` (`reason` `group_mentions_unavailable`) until step 31 adds groups, the two permissions and `group_mentions`.
- **Reply with quote.** `replyToMessageId` names a message of the same conversation; its author is notified. If the original is deleted the quote shows "Bu mesaj silindi".
  - **Server details (step 9).** A reply is refused with `400 messaging.reply_invalid` (`reason` `reply.not_found` for an unknown message, one of another conversation or one the sender cannot read, all with the same answer; `reply.deleted` for a tombstone). Reads carry `replyTo { messageId, authorAccountId, text, deleted }`: `text` is the original's derived text cut at 200 characters, read at read time (an edit shows in the quote at once); a deleted original gives `{ messageId, deleted: true }` only, and clients show "Bu mesaj silindi". The author is told through `messaging.activity-added.v1` (`quoted_reply`), not when replying to oneself, and only if they still read the conversation. A reply that also mentions them still sends `quoted_reply` until the mention feed exists. No live event of its own: clients update a quote from `MessageEdited` and `MessageDeleted`. Behind `Features:MessageReply` (off: `403 messaging.message_reply_unavailable`).
- **Edit and delete.** Unlimited in time, by the author only. Edits show "düzenlendi"; no edit history is kept and a delete erases text, blocks, mentions, links, reactions, pins, saved entries and attachments at once, leaving the tombstone of ADR 0011 ("Bu mesaj silindi"); this follows the requirements' proposal (owner decision 2). Moderators with `messages.remove-others-public` delete others' messages in public channels only. Deleting a thread root keeps its thread under a tombstone.
- **Forward.** Within the organization, to at most 10 targets per call (conversations the forwarder can post to, or accounts, which open DMs), with an optional note (blocks under the message rules, at most 1,000 characters of derived text; `400 messaging.message_invalid`). The forwarder must be able to read the original. A copy is a message whose content is the original's current content (a forward of a forward points to the original); edits and the delete reflect in copies ("İletilen mesaj, sahibi tarafından silindi"). "Orijinal mesaja git" shows only to viewers who can read the original; from a private channel the copy shows "Özel kanal" and no link, and from a DM or group DM "Doğrudan mesaj" and no link. Attachments are shared with the copy's readers. At most 100 copies per original (`409 messaging.forward_limit`). The confirmation names how many people will see it ("# duyurular'daki 120 kişi bu mesajı görecek. Ekli dosya da paylaşılacak.").
- **Reactions.** One Unicode emoji sequence (at most 32 bytes, validated against an emoji list shipped with the server) per reaction; at most 50 distinct emoji per message and 20 per person per message (`409 messaging.reaction_limit`). Messages carry a summary `{ emoji, count, reactedByMe, sample: [accountId ×3] }`.
- **Pins and saved messages.** Pins need `messages.pin`, at most 100 per conversation (`409 messaging.pin_limit`); shown in the conversation's Pinned tab. Saving is personal: at most 5,000 saved items per person (`409 messaging.saved_limit`), with an optional reminder.
- **Seen.** In DMs and group DMs the author sees per person who has seen a message; in channels, a count ("Görüldü · 24"). It is based on `seen_seq`, which only moves forward and is not moved back by "mark unread" (requirements: marking does not take back "seen"). Only the author of the message can ask, and only about their own messages. **Enforcement is in Messaging**, both in `GET .../seen` and when it publishes `SeenChanged`: when a reader's `seen_seq` advances in a DM or group DM, Messaging finds the authors of messages in the newly covered range (one grouped query over `(conversation_id, seq)`, at most 8 authors) and publishes `SeenChanged` only to each such author's **account group**, with `seenUpToSeq` clipped to that author's newest own message in the range. No participant learns how far someone read other people's messages. Channels get no live seen events; the author's client refetches the count at most every 30 seconds while the message is visible.
- **Typing.** A hub call, at most one per 3 seconds per person and conversation, delivered to the conversation's group (except the sender) and never stored.
- **Long text (Phase 1 decision 12).** Over 4,000 characters the composer offers to send the text as a `.txt` attachment (UTF-8, named after the first words, at most 60 characters plus `.txt`, with an optional short message) through flow 6; the server still never accepts more than 4,000 characters in `text`.
- **Rate limits.** Per account and organization: 20 sends per 10 seconds, 30 edits and 30 deletes per minute, 30 reactions per 10 seconds, 10 forwards per minute, 30 poll votes per minute, 30 new DMs or group DMs per hour, 20 new channels per hour, 200 conversation member additions per hour, 6 "mark all read" per minute (`429 messaging.rate_limited` with `Retry-After`).

### 3. Read state, unread and sync (ADR 0011)

- **Read state.** As ADR 0011: automatic reads move forward only and only with the current `version`; explicit "mark read"/"mark unread" (Esc for a conversation, Shift+Esc for all, Alt+click for a message) set the value and bump `version`; a stale automatic read answers `409 messaging.read_state_stale` with the current state; sending advances the sender's own read state. Threads have their own read state. Marking affects only the person's view and syncs across devices.
- **Unread.** Counted from rows, capped at 100 and shown as "99+". The banner "2 yeni mesaj · 10:12'den beri · Okundu say · İlk okunmamışa git" and the "Yeni" line come from `lastReadSeq` as loaded when the conversation opened.
- **Thread followers and the activity feed.** As ADR 0011 (followers: root author, repliers, mentioned people; a person can stop following; feed rows with ids only, kept 30 days). Feed kinds: `thread_reply`, `mention`, `group_mention` (here and groups), `reply` (quoted), `dm_message`, `conversation_added`, `conversation_removed`, `read_state`, `reminder_due`, `scheduled_failed`, `invitation_accepted`.
- **Mute and group-mention settings in counts.** ADR 0011 counts a group mention only if the conversation is not muted and the person has not turned group mentions off. Mute is Messaging's own (`conversation_prefs.muted_until`). "Ignore `@kanal` and `@burada`" is owned by Notifications; Messaging keeps an **event-maintained projection** `messaging.mention_settings`, fed by `notifications.settings-changed.v1`, because the counts run inside the single `LATERAL` query of the snapshot and the sidebar, where a per-row contract call is impossible and a cross-module table read is forbidden. Until the event arrives the previous value applies (lag p95 1 s). Notifications reads mute through `IConversationPrefs` (Messaging.Contracts).
- **Jump to a message.** `GET /conversations/{id}/messages?around={messageId}&limit=` (for links to a message, search results, pins, saved messages and "go to original") answers the message and up to `limit` messages in all (1 to 100, default 50, the anchor included) around it, so the client can open the conversation at that place. `around` is mutually exclusive with `before`, `after` and `changed_after` (`400 messaging.page_invalid`) and takes no cursor, so it needs no `epoch`; a presented `epoch` is still checked (`409 messaging.resync_required`), and `503 cell.epoch_unconfirmed` applies as for the other reads. Answer: `{ items, before, after, anchor: { id, seq }, epoch }` (plus `historyBoundary` once step 20 exists). `items` are newest first with the anchor among them, in the item shape of every other read (a deleted message, the anchor included, is its tombstone in its place; `replyTo` is carried). About half of the other messages are older and half newer (an odd rest gives the extra place to the newer side), and when one side runs out the other fills the places, so the answer holds `limit` messages whenever the conversation has that many. `before` is the `before` cursor that continues to older messages (the `seq` of the oldest message returned, `null` when nothing is older) and `after` the `after` cursor that continues to newer ones (the `seq` of the newest message returned, `null` when nothing is newer); the client passes them to the existing reads together with the `epoch` of the answer. Two keyset queries on `(conversation_id, seq)`, at most `limit` rows each, no offset. An anchor that does not exist, is in another conversation or organization, or is in a conversation the caller cannot read answers one and the same `404 messaging.message_not_found` (no existence oracle; `messaging.conversation_not_found` is not used for this read), and so does an anchor hidden by the history boundary (flow 12). Read-only and additive, so it needs no flag.
- **Sync.** `GET .../sync?since={cursor}` returns the delta in pages; `GET .../sync/snapshot` the full state in pages from one `REPEATABLE READ` snapshot, with the cursor it is consistent with. `pending` and `degraded` as ADR 0011. A cursor that is too old, of another epoch, ahead of the server or malformed answers `409 messaging.resync_required`. Cursors are opaque strings (base64url of epoch and feed `seq`), at most 64 characters.
- **Cell epoch.** The control plane's cell registry holds `epoch`; `Welcome` carries it (new field, additive). The connector stores it; the app role refuses writes with `503 cell.epoch_unconfirmed` (`Retry-After`) while the stored epoch is not confirmed. The restore and failover procedure increments the epoch in the control plane and clears the confirmation in the restored database before the cell starts. _(human review)_
- **Client.** Per cell: cursors, epoch, conversation list and recent messages in a local cache (IndexedDB through `@backroom/local-store` on web and desktop in Phase 2, owner decision 9; SQLite on desktop later) of at most 200 MB, evicting the least recently opened conversations first and keeping at most the newest 1,000 messages per conversation; cleared on sign-out and on removal. Start and switch draw from the cache, then sync. After every reconnect, `sync?since` runs before relying on live events again.

### 4. Polls

- Created from the composer's "+" menu: question (1 to 300 characters), 2 to 10 options (1 to 100 characters each, reorderable), "Birden fazla seçim", "Anonim oylama", "Sonuçlar" (always visible, after voting, after closing; default after voting), "Bitiş" (optional, from 5 minutes to 90 days ahead). A poll without an end time **closes automatically 30 days after creation**. A poll is a message of kind `poll`; it cannot have a thread, be edited or be forwarded.
- Votes can be changed while open; the creator can close early; options cannot change after the first vote (a poll is never edited, so this always holds).
- **Closing job.** `messaging.poll-close` runs as the ADR 0010 maintenance role (`backroom_maintenance`) in the worker, at most 1,000 polls per batch through the partial index on open polls, with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`. Grants in Messaging's migration, under ADR 0010's rules: `SELECT (org_id, message_id, closes_at, created_at, closed_at)` and `UPDATE (closed_at)` on `messaging.polls`; `SELECT (org_id, message_id)` and `DELETE` on `messaging.poll_votes`; `SELECT (id, org_id, conversation_id, version_seq)` and `UPDATE (version_seq)` on `messaging.messages`; `SELECT (id, org_id, change_seq)` and `UPDATE (change_seq)` on `messaging.conversations`; `INSERT` on `messaging.outbox`. No content column (question, option text) is readable. Per poll, in one short transaction: take the conversation's next `change_seq`, set the message's `version_seq` and the poll's `closed_at`, delete the anonymous poll's vote rows (counts stay in `poll_options.vote_count`, maintained on each vote), and write an outbox row whose consumer publishes `PollChanged`. Cascades: none; `poll_votes` has no dependants, and deleting from it cascades nowhere. Its lag alert fires after 10 minutes. How it takes the next number follows ADR 0012.
- **Anonymous polls.** The vote-to-person rows exist only to enforce one vote per person and allow changing it. No API returns them to anyone, the creator and admins included; the voter's own choice is returned only to the voter (owner decision 10). The mapping may sit in encrypted, time-limited backups until the poll closes (accepted by the owner). Closing, by the creator or the job, deletes every vote row of the poll in the same transaction and keeps only the counts.
- **Deleting a poll message** (any poll, anonymous or not) deletes its votes, options and counts in the delete's transaction, leaving the tombstone.
- Non-anonymous polls show voters per option to readers of the conversation.
- Live events carry no counts (`PollChanged { messageId, versionSeq }`), because visibility depends on the viewer; clients read the poll, and the server applies results visibility per viewer.
- Display: "12 kişi oy verdi · Yarın 17:00'de kapanır", "Oyumu değiştir", "Sonuçlandı", "Anonim", "14 oy · Dün 17:00'de kapandı".

### 5. Scheduled messages and reminders

- **Scheduled messages.** Same body as a send (blocks, attachments, reply, thread), at a time from 1 minute to 120 days ahead; at most 100 pending per person. Editable until sent, "send now", delete. Only the author sees them (list "Zamanlanmış mesajlar"). At the time a worker job sends it through the regular send path as the author, rechecking membership and permissions; when that fails the author gets a `scheduled_failed` feed entry and the draft stays for 7 days. Once sent it is a normal message.
- **Off-hours warning.** When the recipient of a DM (or every other person in a group DM) is outside working hours (Phase 2: 22:00–07:00 in their time zone), the composer warns with their local time and offers "mesai başlangıcında gönder" (07:00 their time). The server answers `GET .../dms/{id}/recipient-hours` with only `{ outsideHours, localTime, nextStart }` per person, never the time zone.
- **Reminders.** On a saved message: 20 minutes, 1 hour, tomorrow 09:00, next week (Monday 09:00), or a custom time up to one year ahead, in the person's time zone. When due, a `reminder_due` feed row and a notification; reminders sit in Saved's "Hatırlatıcılar" tab. A reminder on a message the person can no longer read is dropped silently.
- Jobs store their schedule in PostgreSQL (ADR 0006), run in the worker, select due rows with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` through a partial index on pending rows, and use `TimeProvider`.

### 6. Files

1. **Start.** `POST .../files/uploads { conversationId, name, size, contentType }` with `files.upload` and the right to post in the conversation. When the organization has a file quota (flow 12), the declared size is reserved first (`409 files.quota_exceeded` with `{ usedBytes, quotaBytes }`). Rules: size 1 byte to 100 MB (`413 files.too_large`); name NFC, at most 255 characters, no path separators or control characters (`400 files.name_invalid`); content type from an allow-list or `application/octet-stream`. Answers `{ fileId, uploadUrl, headers, expiresAt }`: a presigned PUT valid 15 minutes for exactly that size and content type, to a storage key the server chose (`{orgId}/{fileId}`; the name never appears in keys or URLs).
2. **Upload.** The client uploads straight to object storage through `IFileStorage` (SeaweedFS in development).
3. **Complete.** `POST .../files/{fileId}/complete`: the server checks the object's size, sniffs the type from the first bytes and stores the detected type and a kind (`document`, `image`, `spreadsheet`, `archive`, `other`). Images (at most 50 megapixels) get thumbnails in the worker (64 and 360 px wide, re-encoded WebP); the file is usable before its thumbnails exist. Completion also queues a malware scan behind `IFileScanner` (owner decision 5; ClamAV in development, run as a separate container; the production scanner is chosen in the deploy phase): the file is `scanning` until the scan answers; downloads and attaching wait for `ready` (`409 files.scan_pending`); an infected file becomes `rejected`, its object is deleted and the uploader is told (`files.rejected`). A scanner outage keeps files `scanning` and raises an alert; it never fails open.
4. **Attach.** A send names at most 10 ready, unattached files of the same uploader and conversation (`400 messaging.attachment_invalid`). Unattached uploads are deleted after 24 hours by a maintenance job.
5. **Download.** `GET .../files/{fileId}/download` (and `.../thumbnails/{size}`) rechecks access on every request (Messaging: the person can read a message, or a forwarded copy, that carries the file) and answers `302` to a signed GET URL valid 60 seconds with `Content-Disposition: attachment` and `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` (thumbnails inline, `image/webp`). The signed URL is never cached or logged. Refused and unknown both answer `404 files.not_found`.
6. **Delete.** The uploader, or `files.remove-others-public` in public channels. Deleting the message deletes its attachments. Objects are deleted by a job within an hour.
7. **Conversation tabs.** Files (filters Belgeler, Görseller, Tablolar and "Paylaşan"), Links (from the link rows of messages), Pinned. Each list is keyset-paged, newest first, showing thumbnails only.

**Abuse brakes** (kept alongside the quota of flow 12): 100 MB per file (requirements); per account 30 starts per minute and 10 GB per day; per organization 100 GB per day (`429 files.rate_limited` with `Retry-After`). Files are never served inline (except thumbnails) and never from our web origin.

### 7. Search and the command palette

- **Module.** The Search module (module plan) owns `ISearchIndex`, the search endpoints, query validation, composition of result types, paging, tab counts and rate limits. In Phase 2 its PostgreSQL implementation stores no index of its own: ADR 0011 keeps the `tsvector` on `messaging.messages`, because events carry no text. Each owner exposes a search source in its Contracts that runs the text match and the permission filter in one query in its own schema: `IMessageSearchSource` and `IChannelSearchSource` (Messaging), `IFileSearchSource` (Files), `IMemberSearchSource` (Organization). Search calls them with the authenticated account and never touches their tables. A later engine with its own index replaces the implementation behind `ISearchIndex` and needs its own ADR (it would need text in events or a reindex path).
- **Scope.** Messages, files by name, channels by name (public channels and private channels the person belongs to), people by display name. Rooms come in Phase 3.
- **Query.** `GET .../search?q=&type=&from=&in=&after=&before=&has=&mentionsMe=&threads=&cursor=&limit=`. Typed filters ("kimden:", "nerede:", "tarih:", "dosya:pdf", and their English forms) are parsed by the client into these parameters, so the server never parses localized syntax. Quick filters: has file, has link, mentions me, threads. `q` at most 200 characters and 10 terms. Results newest first, keyset by `(created_at, id)`, 20 per page. Tab counts are capped at 100.
- **Text rules.** NFKC, unaccent, then a fixed fold applied to both the index and the query: `İ`, `I`, `ı` and `i` all fold to `i`, and every other letter is lower-cased with the invariant rule. Prefix matching on the last term. The `simple` text search configuration (no language stemming).
- **Permissions.** As ADR 0011: membership and read access are part of the same SQL query as the match; counts and snippets are computed only over rows that passed. Snippets are returned as plain text with highlight offsets, never markup.
- **Command palette (Ctrl+K).** Client-side commands plus `GET .../search/quick?q=` (channels and people, at most 8 each, prefix match on names). Inaccessible conversations never appear.
- Rate limit: 10 searches per 10 seconds per account (`429 search.rate_limited`).

### 8. Notifications

- **Inputs.** Feed rows for the person (mentions, quoted replies, thread replies, DM messages, additions to conversations, reminders, failed scheduled messages) and the invitation acceptance promised in Phase 1 (to the inviter). Messaging publishes `messaging.activity-added.v1`; Organization publishes acceptance; Notifications decides.
- **Decision order (requirements).** Do not disturb (and, from Phase 4, leave and off-hours) first; then the conversation setting (all messages; mentions and replies; nothing; the unread badge stays); then organization mute; then the account default. Extra settings per conversation: replies in followed threads, ignore `@kanal` and `@burada`, sound. Mute options as the sidebar. Texts as the Thread design ("Varsayılanlar Hesap ayarları › Bildirimler'den gelir. Bu ayar yalnızca # genel için geçerlidir.").
- **Do not disturb is applied last (implementation decision, step 29).** The settings (conversation, organization mute, account default) decide first; do not disturb then turns a delivery into a silent bell entry. It never adds an entry the settings drop: a conversation set to "nothing" must not fill the bell while do not disturb is on, and it would surprise the person when do not disturb ends. "First" in the order above means it wins over delivery, not over "nothing".
- **Outputs.** A bell entry (tabs Tümü and Bahsedilmeler in Phase 2; "Tümünü okundu say"), and, unless suppressed, a `NotificationRequested` live event to the person's account group, which the web app shows with the browser's Notification API and the desktop app with the operating system's notifications (the desktop app keeps running in the tray when its window closes). Under do not disturb, entries collect in the bell silently ("Rahatsız etme açıkken bildirimler burada birikir.").
- **Content.** Bell rows store ids only; the preview is read through the regular permission check when shown. The live event carries a preview of at most 150 characters, only to that person; an account setting hides previews in operating system notifications.
- **Web Push** (owner decision 6; opt-in, last step of Phase 2): a person turns it on per browser; the subscription (endpoint and keys) is stored per account and device in Notifications and deleted when they turn it off, sign out, or the push service answers 404/410. Push payloads carry no content and no names: only `{ orgId, notificationId, kind }`; the service worker shows a generic text ("Yeni bildirim") and the app loads the details after the person opens it. VAPID keys are an AppHost secret parameter. Outside production, pushes go only to a local fake push endpoint (the outbound-channel rule of ADR 0008); real push services need Production and an explicit setting.
- **Never** for a conversation the person cannot read, never for their own messages, at most one notification per message per person.

### 9. Presence and status (Phase 2 base)

- **Automatic presence.** Online, idle (10 minutes without input, reported by the client), offline (no connection to the cell for 60 seconds). Computed per cell from the person's hub connections and kept in Valkey with expiry; never stored in PostgreSQL.
- **Manual status.** Do not disturb (30 minutes, 1 hour, until tomorrow, custom) and a custom status (one emoji, at most 100 characters, optional expiry) are account-level, so they look the same in every organization. They live in the control plane (Identity), are set with `PUT /api/v1/me/status`, and reach every cell where the account is a member through the link (`AccountStatusChanged`, versioned). The cell keeps its copy in Valkey (key per account, outside RLS, owner decision 16; restored by `FullSync`); the organization context comes from authentication, and `IPresenceReader` answers only for accounts that are members of the caller's organization. The time zone (IANA id reported by the client) travels the same way and is used only for reminders, mute "until tomorrow" and the off-hours warning; no endpoint returns it to others.
- **Away chosen by hand** (owner decision of 2026-11-25, below). A person can set "Boşta" themselves. It is account-level like do not disturb: stored in the control plane next to it (`away`), set with the same `PUT /api/v1/me/status`, and sent to the cells in `AccountStatusChanged` (the additive field `away`) and the status sync after `FullSync`. It has no end and stays until the person picks "Çevrimiçi" or do not disturb. A status with do not disturb is stored with away cleared, so away does not come back when do not disturb ends. It does not mute notifications. The shown state, by precedence: offline (no connection) > do not disturb > away chosen by hand > automatic idle > online. Presence payloads keep their states: the cell shows an online person with the flag as `idle`, so others cannot tell it from the automatic one and nothing new is exposed; only the owner's own `GET /api/v1/me/status` returns `away`. In the web menu "Boşta" can be chosen, and "Çevrimiçi" clears both away and do not disturb.
- **Timed statuses end on the client.** The server sends no event when do not disturb or a custom status reaches its end; it leaves expired values out whenever it is asked. A client therefore has to look again by itself when such a time passes. The web does this for the person's own status today: one shared timer (`expiry-clock.ts`, `useExpiryClock`) waits for the nearest end, draws the menu again and asks for the own status again. The web has no view of other people's presence yet; when one arrives (member lists, DMs) it must read its entries through `currentPresence` and register their ends (`presenceExpiries`) with the same clock, so there is still one timer.
- **Activity labels** ("Toplantıda", "Ekran paylaşıyor") arrive with meetings in Phase 3.
- **Watching.** A client asks for presence of the people it shows (at most 200 per connection, through the hub) and gets changes through per-person presence groups; changes are coalesced to at most one per 5 seconds per person. Presence never shows platform or device.
- **Limits fixed in step 28.** Do not disturb ends within 7 days; a custom status ends within 30 days and has one emoji (a single grapheme cluster), up to 100 characters (grapheme clusters, NFC, trimmed, no control or format characters except joiners) or both; the time zone is an IANA id. `GET /presence` takes the ids comma-separated. Live payloads carry timestamps as ISO 8601 strings. The hub's receive limit rises to 16 KB for `WatchPresence`'s 200 ids. A person's changes reach only the organizations where someone watches them (watches expire after 10 minutes unless the watching node renews them), and `organization.member-removed.v1` stops them at once.

### 10. Audit log and moderation

- **Audit log.** Append-only (`INSERT` and `SELECT` only for the app role; no update or delete path) and organization-scoped. Phase 2 records: role assignments; member added, removed, deactivated, reactivated; invitation created, resent, revoked, accepted, failed (Phase 1 promise); public channel created, renamed, archived, unarchived; private channel created, archived, ownership transferred (ids and member counts, **never names**); DMs and group DMs not at all; messages removed by moderators (ids only); reports filed and resolved; groups changed. Login and security events of the control plane stay security log events until Phase 5. No content in any entry. Viewing needs `audit.view`; keyset-paged, newest first, filter by action and actor. Entries are kept 1 year (owner decision 8; maintenance job `audit.retention`).
- **Channel names in the audit log.** Audit stores conversation ids only and resolves names **at read time** through `IConversationNames.GetPublicNamesAsync(ids)` (Messaging.Contracts), which returns names only for public channels (any member can read those anyway) and `null` for every other kind, so the privacy decision stays in the module that owns the kinds. The alternative, a public name in the event, was rejected: it breaks the ids-only rule for events, needs a per-kind exception that a later change could get wrong, and copies names into a second store. Trade-off: entries show the current name, not the name at the time; a rename entry records only that a rename happened.
- **Reporting.** "Mesajı bildir" by anyone who can read the message: reason (spam, harassment, inappropriate, other) and an optional note (at most 500 characters, the reporter's own words); at most 20 reports per person per day and one open report per person and message. Moderators never get a copy of message content: the owner chose metadata only (owner decision 14), because a copy from a private channel, DM or group DM would conflict with "no role reads others' messages". no text, blocks, attachment names or conversation name are stored. For a public channel the report holds the message id, conversation id, author, reporter, reason, note and time, and moderators open the message through the regular read (they can read public channels anyway). For a private channel, DM or group DM, moderators see only the reported author, the reporter, the reason, the note and the time: no message id, conversation id, kind, participants or link, so a report never reveals that a DM exists between two people or which private channel it came from (gate scenario 1). The report row keeps the message id for de-duplication and for deleting the report when the message is deleted, but no moderator API returns it.
- **Review.** Moderators list open reports, dismiss one, or act: delete the message (public channels only), remove or deactivate the author (flow 11). Every resolution is audited. Report records are deleted 90 days after resolution (owner decision 8; maintenance job `audit.report-retention`).
- **Delivery status of invitations** (Phase 1 promise): the invitation list gains `deliveryStatus` (`pending`, `sent`, `failed`, `unknown`) from Email's delivery log.

### 11. Member removal, deactivation and user groups

- **Remove** (`members.remove`): the person leaves the organization, loses every conversation membership, and may return by a new invitation; their messages stay with their name. **Deactivate** (`members.deactivate`; reactivate likewise): the person cannot return by invitation; their open sessions in that organization end at once (the requirements' "açık oturumları hemen kapanır"): in the commit's transaction the membership becomes inactive, so every request to the organization answers the membership check's 404 from then on, and after commit Realtime removes the account's connections from **every group of that organization only** (organization, conversation, account and presence groups, all prefixed `org:{orgId}`) on every node, through the existing `realtime:member-removed` broadcast and the `ConnectionRegistry`'s per-connection group list, and sends those connections `OrganizationAccessEnded { orgId, reason: "deactivated" }`. The connections stay open for the account's other organizations on the same cell, and re-subscribing to the deactivated organization is refused by the membership check. Cell tokens are scoped to a cell, not to an organization, so no token can be revoked for one organization alone; the membership check on every request and hub call is the organization-scoped "session end". If the broadcast is lost, the bound is the token lifetime (5 minutes), as for removal. Keycloak sessions and the person's other organizations are not touched; the owner confirmed this scope (owner decision 15). Their messages stay labelled "deaktif", DMs with them become read-only. Owner cannot be removed or deactivated; nobody acts on a role higher than their own (requirements' proposal). Both publish `organization.member-removed.v1` or `organization.member-deactivated.v1`, revoke their pending invitations in the same transaction (Phase 1 flow 3 step 8) and update the control plane through `MembershipChanged`. _(human review)_
- **User groups** (`members.manage-groups`): name (`DisplayText`, at most 80, unique per organization), at most 1,000 members, at most 500 groups per organization. Phase 2 uses them only for group mentions ("@tasarım-ekibi"); floor access and calendar scopes come later.

### 12. Plan limits: visible history and file quota (owner decision 4)

- **Settings.** Plans do not exist yet, so Phase 2 adds two organization settings owned by Organization: `visibleHistoryDays` (`null` = unlimited, else 1 to 3,650) and `fileQuotaBytes` (`null` = unlimited), both unlimited by default. When plans arrive, the free plan maps to 14 days and 5 GB and paid plans to unlimited. Organization members cannot change them: operations set them through the control plane (`PUT /api/v1/ops/organizations/{id}/limits`), audited twice: in the control plane's ops audit log with the operator's account id, and, when the cell applies the message, as an `organization.limits-changed` entry in the organization's own audit log (actor "operations", no operator identity), which sends `OrganizationLimitsChanged` (versioned) over the link; Licensing sends the same message later. Organization publishes `organization.limits-changed.v1` and Realtime pushes `HistoryBoundaryChanged` to the organization group. _(human review)_
- **Nothing is deleted.** Older messages stay stored and become visible again at once when the limit is lifted ("Mesaj geçmişi sınırsızdır" stays true for storage).
- **Data rights are never limited by plan visibility** ("Decisions beyond the requirements", 33). Hidden messages and files are stored personal data. A person's personal-data export includes their own hidden messages and files, and the erasure tools (account deletion and anonymization, and the data-rights erasure of one's own messages) cover their hidden messages too. Deleting a hidden message from the normal UI is not possible, because it is not shown. Organization-level exports do not exist in Phase 2; when they arrive they follow plan visibility. The export and erasure tools themselves are not in Phase 2, so Phase 2 fixes the rule in their data sources: Messaging and Files expose `IPersonalDataSource` (their Contracts), which ignores plan visibility and is tested now; the tools build on it later.
- **Visible from and the boundary.** `visible_from = now − visibleHistoryDays` per organization (from `TimeProvider`; `-infinity` when unlimited). Per conversation, `visibleFromSeq` is the smallest `seq` whose message has `created_at >= visible_from`, found by one probe of the `(conversation_id, created_at)` index (Data). **Every read path compares `seq` only**, never `created_at`: `created_at` is taken before commit and clocks differ between nodes, so it is not commit-ordered, and a time filter could hide a message whose neighbour by `seq` is shown, leaving a gap that catch-up would treat as missing. `visibleFromSeq` is cached per conversation and node for at most 60 seconds and recomputed when the cache expires, on `HistoryBoundaryChanged`, and whenever a read finds it would move (it only grows while the limit is unchanged).
- **Enforced on the server, in every read path,** as `seq >= visibleFromSeq` in the same query as the read (never by the client):
  - the conversation list and All DMs: the preview, `last_message_at` and the sort key come from the newest **visible** message; when every message is hidden the row shows the "no visible messages" state ("Görünür mesaj yok") and sorts by `created_at`;
  - the snapshot's open mentions and mention counters, `GET .../seen` and `GET .../polls/{messageId}` (and poll voter lists) treat hidden messages as unknown;
  - history paging, `changed_after` catch-up and sync (changes to hidden messages are not returned; the boundary marker is);
  - threads: replies by their own time; a hidden root shows the placeholder;
  - search: the index keeps hidden rows, results, counts and snippets filter them in the same query;
  - files: a file is visible only if its **original** message (the one it was uploaded to) is visible; a forwarded copy never makes a hidden original's files visible; file lists, the Links and Pinned tabs, file search and downloads (`404 files.not_found`) filter hidden ones;
  - forwarded copies: visible by their own time; when the original is hidden the copy shows a placeholder ("Bu mesaj mevcut planda görünmüyor") and no attachments, never its content; a quote of a hidden message likewise;
  - pins, saved items and reminders (a reminder on a hidden message is dropped silently), the activity feed (rows pointing into hidden messages are left out, so no link leads there), the bell and notifications (none for hidden content);
  - writes that target a hidden message (edit, delete, react, reply, forward, pin, save, vote) answer `404 messaging.message_not_found`, as for an unknown message.
- **Unread counts** ignore hidden messages: the count starts at `GREATEST(last_read_seq, visibleFromSeq − 1)`.
- **Plan change.** Lowering or lifting `visibleHistoryDays` publishes `HistoryBoundaryChanged { orgId, visibleFrom }`; clients rerun sync after a random delay of 0 to 30 seconds (so an organization's clients do not resync at once), which returns the new boundary per conversation, and refetch history when it moved back. The boundary also moves with time; every read returns the current one.
- **File quota** (`fileQuotaBytes`): race-safe reservation. The `org_usage` row is created when the organization is provisioned (`INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, also run by a migration for existing organizations), and upload start, in one transaction, upserts it so a missing row can never let an upload through: `INSERT INTO files.org_usage (org_id, used_bytes, reserved_bytes) VALUES ($org, 0, 0) ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, then `UPDATE files.org_usage SET reserved_bytes = reserved_bytes + $size WHERE org_id = $org AND ($quota IS NULL OR used_bytes + reserved_bytes + $size <= $quota)` (a null quota is unlimited; the row lock serializes concurrent starts); no row updated → `409 files.quota_exceeded { usedBytes, quotaBytes }`. Reservations are tracked even without a quota, so a quota set later starts from correct numbers. Completion checks the object's real size equals the declared size (else `409 files.upload_incomplete` and the object is deleted) and moves the bytes from reserved to used. Rejected (malware) uploads release their reservation at once. Expired reservations (upload not completed within 24 hours) are released by the maintenance job `files.reservation-release` (ADR 0010 role, batches of 1,000 with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`): grants `SELECT (id, org_id, status, reserved_bytes, created_at)` and `UPDATE (status, reserved_bytes)` on `files.files`, `SELECT (org_id)` and `UPDATE (reserved_bytes)` on `files.org_usage`; no content columns (names) and no cascades. Each batch subtracts the released bytes in the same transaction that marks the uploads `expired`. Deleting a file frees its bytes at once, in the delete's transaction (the object is removed later). Thumbnails do not count (they are ours and small). Files attached only to hidden messages still count. Lowering the quota below usage deletes nothing; new uploads are refused until usage falls.
- **Admin view and transparency.** `GET .../settings/limits` shows members both limits and, with `organization.manage-settings`, the usage (`usedBytes`, `quotaBytes`). The conversation shows at its boundary "Ücretsiz planda son 14 günün mesajları görünür" (with the organization's day count until plans exist); upload start returns `usage` when the quota is set, and at 80 % or more the composer shows it ("5 GB'ın 4,3 GB'ı kullanılıyor").
- **Abuse brakes** stay: 100 MB per file and the upload rate limits of flow 6. They limit abuse, not storage.
- **Hidden files** (owner decision 17): a file attached to a hidden message is hidden too, in file lists, search and links, but still counts toward the quota.

**Limits.** Groups per connection rise from 200 to 600 (conversations plus presence groups). A client subscribes to every conversation in its sidebar and the open one; conversations beyond the limit are refreshed by sync when the window regains focus. **Removal** from a conversation is published after commit on `realtime:conversation-member-removed` (every node drops that account's connections from the group) and again by the consumer of `messaging.conversation-member-removed.v1`; if both broadcasts are lost the bound is the token lifetime (5 minutes), as for organization removal; accepted by the owner (owner decision 12), and the read APIs refuse at once. **Window after the commit (step 2 review):** even when the broadcast arrives, eviction runs on its own Valkey subscription, apart from the SignalR backplane, so a conversation event published in the milliseconds after the removal commit can still reach the removed account's connections; there is no delivery-time membership check. The web client narrows this by delivering conversation events only for conversations it still wants (after its own leave, or after `MembershipChanged { change: "removed" }` for itself), which covers honest clients only. The durable consumer skips the eviction when the account is a member again (a late event must not cut off a member who rejoined); reading a public channel alone does not count. **Removal by others** (private channel owners removing members, organization removal, deactivation) must not ship until there is a delivery-time membership check, or the owner has accepted this window again for it. _(human review)_

## HTTP APIs

JSON, `/api/v1`, bearer token, problem details, generated OpenAPI snapshot-tested with Verify (Phase 1). Every cell route below is under `/api/v1/orgs/{orgId}` and runs the membership check first. ## Decisions in this contract that go beyond the requirements

1. Phase 2 includes private channels, the rich text editor, sidebar sections and All DMs (Phase 1 deferred them), and member removal, deactivation and user groups (moderation and group mentions need them).
2. Channels become conversations in place (`channels` → `conversations`, `channel_id` → `conversation_id`); message routes move to `/conversations/{id}/...` and the Phase 1 channel message routes are removed in the same PR as the web client's switch (no other clients exist).
3. Clients send rich text as our block schema; the server validates strictly, re-serializes and derives `text`, mentions and links. This refines Phase 1 decision 12 ("never trusts client-supplied blocks") from "derives blocks from text" to "validates and canonicalizes blocks".
4. Public channels have explicit membership (join and leave), which decides the sidebar and unread counts; reading and searching need only organization membership.
5. A group DM's identity is its original set of people; the same set reopens it; it is converted to a private channel in place, keeping history.
6. Threads exist only under roots in channels, DMs and group DMs; "Kanala da gönder" is a reference row in the parent, not a copy.
7. Forwarded copies show the original's current content, so edits and deletes reflect (the requirements' rule), share its attachments, and are capped at 100 per original; DM sources are labelled "Doğrudan mesaj" without a link, like private channels.
8. `read_states` gains `seen_seq` (forward only), because "mark unread" must not take back "seen"; seen data is shown only to the message's author.
9. Live events for threads go to the parent conversation's group; personal events go to a per-account group; presence goes to per-person groups watched by viewers.
10. Poll live events carry no counts; the server applies results visibility per viewer on read.
11. The search fold maps `İ`, `I`, `ı`, `i` to `i` for both index and query (better recall for Turkish, and the same for every language); typed filters are parsed by the client.
12. Manual status (do not disturb, custom status) and time zone are account-level in the control plane and reach cells over the link; automatic presence is per cell.
13. Bell entries store ids only; previews are read through the permission check, and live notification events carry at most 150 characters only to the recipient.
14. Downloads are always attachments from storage through 60-second signed URLs; image thumbnails are re-encoded WebP (SkiaSharp, MIT; ImageSharp is excluded by the license rules).
15. The cell epoch is delivered in `Welcome`, and writes are fenced until it is confirmed; restore procedures clear the confirmation.
16. Reports carry metadata only (owner decision 14).
17. Audit entries carry ids only; public channel names are resolved at read time through Messaging.Contracts (flow 10), private kinds never resolve; DMs and group DMs are not audited.
18. Search follows the module plan as its own module behind `ISearchIndex`, but owns no index in Phase 2: ADR 0011 keeps the text index in Messaging (events carry no text), so Search composes permission-filtered search sources that each owner runs in its own schema. The module plan's "Arama dizini" becomes Search's only when a separate engine arrives (own ADR).
19. Private channel names are not unique and public name checks ignore private channels, so name conflicts reveal nothing.
20. Seen events go only to the affected authors' account groups, clipped to their own messages; the fan-out rule lives in Messaging.
21. Group-mention settings reach Messaging's counts through an event-maintained projection (`mention_settings`), not a per-row contract call.
22. Polls without an end time close after 30 days; closing and deleting erase vote rows.
23. Deactivation ends the account's access to that organization at once (membership check and hub connections on the cell); other organizations are unaffected (owner decision 15).
24. Presence's `account_status` is an RLS exemption (account-level data), guarded by `IPresenceReader`.
25. Off-hours in Phase 2 is the default 22:00–07:00 window in the recipient's time zone, until work hours arrive in Phase 4.
26. Limits not in the requirements: forward targets 10, mentions 50, reactions 50 distinct and 20 per person, pins 100, saved 5,000, scheduled 100, sections 50, memberships 10,000, group size 1,000, poll closing 5 minutes to 90 days, rate limits as stated.
27. New feature flags, off by default outside development until the gate passes: `Features:RichText`, `Features:Threads`, `Features:Files`, `Features:Search`, `Features:Notifications`, `Features:Presence`, `Features:Polls`, `Features:Scheduling`, `Features:Moderation`, `Features:PlanLimits`, `Features:WebPush`. The conversation routes (list, messages, join, leave) have no flag: they replace the Phase 1 channel routes in step 3.
28. **Plan limits on visibility, not storage** (owner decision 4). The requirements' "Mesaj geçmişi sınırsızdır" stays true for storage: nothing is deleted. On the free plan (once plans exist) only the last 14 days are visible and files have a 5 GB quota per organization; paid plans have neither. Phase 2 implements both as organization settings set by operations, unlimited by default. Hidden messages are still stored personal data, so deletion, anonymization and export rules apply to them. The requirements document ("Planlar ve fiyatlar", "Mesajlaşma", "Dosyalar") needs a matching update.
29. Visibility is enforced on the server in every read path with a per-organization `visible_from`, and clients get a per-conversation `historyBoundary` marker, so ADR 0011's cursors and permanent tombstones are unchanged.
30. Uploads are scanned behind `IFileScanner` (ClamAV in development as a separate container); files are unusable until clean and the scanner never fails open.
31. Web Push is opt-in per browser and carries no content or names; outside production it goes only to a local fake endpoint.
32. Account status in a cell lives in Valkey outside RLS (no `presence` schema), guarded by `IPresenceReader`.
33. **Data rights are not plan-limited.** A person's export includes their own hidden messages and files, and erasure covers them; the normal UI cannot delete a hidden message because it does not show it; organization exports (none in Phase 2) follow plan visibility.
34. The visibility boundary is a per-conversation `seq` (computed from `created_at` once, cached 60 seconds), because `created_at` is not commit-ordered.
